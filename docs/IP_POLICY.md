## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy explains how Q-Quest treats copyright-eligible project materials, third-party components, permission requests, contributions, and infringement reports. It is intended to make the repository’s licensing position clear; it is not legal advice or a government registration.

Subject to originality, authorship, and applicable law, the proprietary claim covers project-specific source-code expression, documentation, written educational content, interface expression, and visual material created for Q-Quest by the identified owner. Each claim is limited to the rights actually held.

The claim does not extend to third-party material, public-domain material, facts, abstract ideas, principles, mathematical concepts, methods or algorithms as such, ordinary functionality, independently developed similar work, or material that is otherwise ineligible for copyright protection.

## 2. Proprietary source-code policy

Q-Quest is not offered under an open-source license. The repository-level `LICENSE` reserves rights in eligible proprietary material, and the npm package is marked `private` and `UNLICENSED` to reduce accidental publication. Those metadata values support, but do not replace, the license text.

Access to the public repository or deployed website does not grant a general right to copy, modify, redistribute, sublicense, host, sell, or commercially exploit proprietary material. Ordinary browser access and uses required or permitted by law remain unaffected.

## 3. Authorized reuse and permissions

Requests must be emailed to the public contact and should include:

- the exact files or material requested;
- the proposed use, audience, distribution channel, and duration;
- whether the use is commercial, educational, or non-commercial;
- the requested right to modify, redistribute, or sublicense; and
- the requester’s name and contact details.

Silence, repository access, a pending pull request, or an unacknowledged request is not authorization. Permission exists only to the extent stated in an express written authorization from the rights holder.

Maintain a durable copy of each granted permission with its date, scope, conditions, and relevant project revision. Do not broaden a permission beyond its stated terms.

## 4. Third-party exclusions and compliance

Third-party software, fonts, icons, photographs, illustrations, feeds, hosted content, templates, service-generated output, trademarks, and other external material remain governed by their respective rights and terms. They are not converted into proprietary Q-Quest material by inclusion in the repository or application.

Before adding external material:

1. record its source, creator, retrieval date, version, and license or permission;
2. retain any required license and attribution text;
3. confirm that the intended web, source, and commercial uses are permitted; and
4. add or update `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

Unknown provenance is not proof of ownership. Material with unresolved provenance should not receive an unqualified ownership claim and should be replaced or cleared before commercial distribution.

## 7. Copyright, trademarks, and patents

- **Copyright** generally protects eligible original expression, including computer programs and artwork, not the underlying idea, method, procedure, or mathematical concept. The Government of India’s [Copyright Handbook](https://copyright.gov.in/Documents/handbook.html) states that protection is automatic and ideas are not protected; [WIPO’s copyright guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/protection) explains the same international baseline.
- **Trademark law** concerns signs that identify the source of goods or services. A copyright notice does not register or establish exclusive trademark rights in the Q-Quest name. Branding clearance and any trademark strategy require a separate assessment.
- **Patent law** concerns qualifying inventions and generally depends on an application and examination. These repository notices do not create patent rights and make no patent claim. Public disclosure can affect patent options, so obtain qualified advice before disclosing a potentially patentable invention.

## 8. Evidence preservation

Keep Git history, signed release tags where practical, original design/source files, dated drafts, prompts and edit records for generated assets, dependency lockfiles, deployment records, and copies of licenses and permissions. Back up the repository in at least two independently controlled locations. Evidence can support a claim but does not guarantee a legal outcome.

Do not rewrite published history merely to improve an evidence record. Correct mistakes with normal follow-up commits and document the reason.
